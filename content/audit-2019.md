## What the 2019 deck got right, and what it missed

The 2019 deck saw a lot that is still live in 2026, and several of these are sharper than it expected: the workforce gap from retirements, WUI growth, family-forest parcelization, social-media crowding and inexperienced visitors, nature as medicine, virtual nature, shrinking snow, fragile trust in science, and Indigenous empowerment. It missed five things. First, wildfire as the force shaping forest policy, including smoke deaths, insurers pulling back and home-hardening rules. Second, how big and fast federal budget, workforce and structural shocks would be, and how far policy would swing between administrations. Third, partnerships becoming the way the work gets done (Good Neighbor, shared stewardship, Tribal co-stewardship), not an optional extra. Fourth, how exposed wood markets are to trade disputes and tariffs. Fifth, how quickly AI would reach visitors and staff. About a third of the cards chased tech novelties far from the mission (fusion, cultured meat, power floors, edible drones).
