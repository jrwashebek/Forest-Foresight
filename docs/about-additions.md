## What changed from the 2019 edition

- **You play as yourself.** Players take leader and partner roles (State Forester, Forest Supervisor, Tribal Natural Resources Director, County Commissioner, and others) instead of "jobs from the future". The jobs live on as the future titles revealed at the end.
- **Conditions, not cubes.** Each of ten domains has a condition from 0 (Crisis) to 10 (Transformed), so a signal can make things better or worse. The 2019 cubes measured only the rate of change.
- **New domains for today's forestry.** Fire & Fuels, Policy & Funding, Workforce & Capacity and Partnerships & Co-Stewardship replace Transportation, Energy, Medicine & Wellness and Food & Agriculture, which fold into the remaining domains.
- **Three eras: Now, Near, Far.** Each era draws signals weighted to its horizon, so the game moves from today's facts to weak signals and wildcards.
- **Leaders decide.** Each era brings an Era Driver with a dilemma for the table, and a Leadership Council where players pool scarce Capacity into strategies. That Capacity shrinks when Workforce or Policy & Funding is strained.
- **Shared stakes.** Each player has a secret mandate, but if the forest system unravels nobody wins. In balance testing, cooperative tables reached a resilient future about nine times as often as selfish ones.
- **A record you can use.** Headline of an Era stays, and now comes with a "So what for us now?" line. The debrief collects the headlines, decisions and signals to watch into a report you can take back to work.
- **A fresh deck.** 98 signals from 2025–2026 sources replace the 2016–2019 deck. 11 of the strongest 2019 cards were refreshed with current evidence.

## Keeping this tool current

- **Every six months: horizon scan.** Add 10–20 new signals in the Content Library and retire ones that have played out. Each card needs a real source you have read, written in your own words.
- **Use the "Review due" filter.** It lists cards not reviewed in 18 months. Re-check each source, update the date, then save or retire the card.
- **After big events, update the drivers.** Examples are a new law, a reorganization or a record fire year. Era Drivers and their dilemmas should reflect choices leaders face now, and scenarios set the starting conditions.
- **Keep it balanced.** Aim for an even mix of good and bad news in each domain, and for trade-offs on about half the cards. If games keep ending the same way, adjust scenario starting levels before rewriting cards.
- **Share your edits.** Edits stay on your device until you choose Publish to everyone, where that's available, or export the JSON and share the file. Phones compute secret mandates from the same content, so everyone at the table must use the same edition. The edition code appears on the host and on every phone.
- **Stay candid and even-handed.** Describe policy changes neutrally with sources, show both opportunities and risks, and never mock people or institutions.
